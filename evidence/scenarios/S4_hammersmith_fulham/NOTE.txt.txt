Scenario 4, Hammersmith and Fulham. Note on scope.

The ICO's reprimand recommended that all material intended for publication be
signed off by a manager, and that a checklist be used when releasing
information containing Excel spreadsheets. The Disclosure Workflow is that
recommendation implemented as a system control rather than as a written
procedure.

What the replay demonstrates: a release that nobody had reviewed was stopped
before publication, and a named officer required the check, with the grounds
recorded and timestamped.

What it does not demonstrate: the artefact cannot open the file and cannot
detect hidden workbooks. In the real case the published response contained an
Excel spreadsheet with 35 hidden workbooks, ten of which held personal
information on 6,528 people including 2,342 children. The ICO accepted that
their existence was not apparent, while noting that anyone with knowledge of
Excel could have inspected the file for hidden data.

The artefact creates the point at which someone must confirm the inspection
happened, and records who required it. Claiming detection would be an
overstatement and would not survive questioning.
